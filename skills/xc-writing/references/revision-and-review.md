# Revision and review

Use this guidance when comparing an existing passage with a revision, or when a writing problem may conceal a change of meaning.

## Establish what must remain true

Read the target with its supplied evidence and requirements. Identify the actor, action, object, and intended reader task. Then attend to the commitments: what is required, permitted, prohibited, conditional, uncertain, or outside scope. A smoother sentence is not an improvement if it changes any of these relationships.

Compare before and after at the level of claims. Watch especially for “only,” “all,” “unless,” “may,” “must,” and their equivalents in the working language. These words are not a blacklist; they carry scope that is easy to lose. “A is required for B” does not mean A alone permits B. An observed absence of errors does not mean errors are impossible. A check on a declared artifact does not necessarily constrain every way someone could write a file.

Keep exact material separate from surrounding prose. Do not normalize spelling, punctuation, capitalization, or paths inside a literal command or log when the task requires exact quotation. A source path supplied as example text remains example text, not a read request.

## Repair the cause of the difficulty

An unclear passage may lack an actor, omit a causal step, introduce terminology too early, or combine several decisions. Diagnose that cause before replacing words. Recover an action from a nominalization when it helps: “The scheduler selects a worker” says more directly what happens than “Worker selection is undertaken by the scheduler.” A nominalization can still be useful when the action itself is the subject of analysis.

Review findings should identify the passage, the likely misunderstanding, and the consequence. Offer a local rewrite when meaning is stable. When it is not stable, name the competing readings and ask the decision needed to distinguish them. Follow the caller's severity, evidence, and closure format; this writing method does not determine overall product acceptance.

## Original examples

### Explain without broadening a guarantee

Source: “The validator rejects a submitted attachment if its digest differs from the declared digest. It does not inspect attachments that are never submitted.”

Revision: “For each submitted attachment, the validator compares its digest with the declared digest and rejects a mismatch. Attachments that are never submitted are outside this check.”

The actor, comparison, rejection, and submitted-only scope remain visible. “The validator ensures attachment integrity” would erase the boundary and overstate what the source supports. A reviewer can ask: does this check cover an attachment that was not submitted? The answer must remain no.

### Keep a permission separate from its prerequisites

Source: “A reviewer may reopen a closed issue after the reporter supplies new evidence, unless the issue is marked legally restricted. Reopening must retain the original issue ID.”

Revision: “After the reporter supplies new evidence, a reviewer may reopen a closed issue unless it is marked legally restricted. The reopened issue must retain its original ID.”

The revision retains the permitted actor, prerequisite, exception, and obligation. It does not say new evidence requires reopening or grants another actor permission. Before accepting it, ask who may reopen, when reopening is prohibited, and whether a new ID is allowed.

### Expose an ambiguity instead of settling it

Source: “Publish the report after review by the analyst and manager or delegate.”

The sentence does not establish whether the delegate replaces the manager alone or can replace both reviewers. A polished sentence that chooses either reading would decide the policy. Report the two readings and ask which roles must review before proposing a final revision.

### Preserve uncertainty in scientific explanation

Source: “In the measured trials, the filter reduced false positives. Its effect on missed detections was not measured.”

Revision: “The filter produced fewer false positives in the measured trials. The trials did not measure whether it also changed the number of missed detections.”

This clarifies the unmeasured outcome without implying it increased, decreased, or remained unchanged. A recommendation to deploy the filter would need a separate judgment about that uncertainty.

## Test understanding within the evidence limits

Give an independent reviewer the source, revision, and relevant reader task. Ask them to explain who does what, under which conditions, and what remains unknown. For an operational explanation, ask what they would do in a concrete case; for a theoretical explanation, ask how the mechanism produces the result. Compare the answer with the source commitments, not with a preferred phrase.

Record material omissions or changed meaning and revise the affected passage. An Agent review can reveal a problem; agreement among Agents does not establish that human readers understand the text. Formula scores, word counts, and structural checks can flag candidates for review but cannot close semantic findings.
